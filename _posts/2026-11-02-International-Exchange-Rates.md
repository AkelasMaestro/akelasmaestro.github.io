Observations and comparisons between Brazilian and American finances.

## Earn in Dollar, Spend in Dollar

While I was visiting my in-laws in Brazil over the summer, one of the popular points of conversation I could participate in was money. Specifically in comparing lifestyles between [America](2026-05-04-all-american.md#) and Brazil. The median income in our respective countries is actually very similar when looking at the number amount in the bank. Things only get weird when you account for the exchange rate.

The [median income](https://www.researchtesseract.com/brazil) in Brazil is BR$42,477 per adult annually (The local currency, real, is pronounced hey-al, and also uses the $ sign). The [median income](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/mepainusa672n) in the U.S. is US$46,960 per adult annually. This comes to about BR$3,540 a month and US$3,913. Please keep in mind that these numbers are broad approximations. When I was in Brazil, I was able to find a government source that claimed that the monthly income (Brazilians don't talk of money in terms of annual income like Americans, it's always monthly) was closer to BR$3,300 and my in-laws and extended family felt that was a reasonable number. I can't readily find that government site, probably because Google knows I'm in the US and only gives me sources in English, and I cannot be bothered to track it down. My main point is that by the numbers, it is easy to compare Brazilian to American expenses since we earn similar number amounts.

Below, I've put a table together to compare the cost of common, local items. These are very rough estimates based on my three months in Brazil and my lifetime in the U.S.. Notice that pricing is similar for local goods. 

| Name            | Value (BR$ ) | Value (US$ ) |
| --------------- | ------------ | ------------ |
| Monthly Income  | $3,500       | $3,900       |
| Rent            | $700-1200    | $1200-1500   |
| Family Barbeque | $50          | $50          |
| Fresh bread     | $5           | $7           |
| kg of dry rice  | $6           | $4           |

 Things get more complicated when you start looking at exchange rates and international goods. The exchange rate between BR$ and US$ is about 5 to 1. Before going on this trip, my wife and I were living off of US$1,000 a month as poor college students (Living with family has been critical). By American standards, I was making about a quarter of the median income and readily labeled poor. By Brazilians standards, I am making BR$5,000, well above the median income.
